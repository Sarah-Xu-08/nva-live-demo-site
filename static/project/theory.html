At step t, st is the shared state, including task context and relevant history; atH and atAI are the human and AI actions. The environment has a transition model T. st+1 is the observed state after the joint action, and gtAI|H is the task expected of the AI by the human. Instructions and reviewed demonstrations establish that task; erroneous AI behavior does not redefine it.

RPE · Task mismatch

A binary task-alignment reward evaluates whether the AI contribution supports the expected task.

rt+1G=RGAI|H(st,atH,atAI,st+1;gtAI|H)∈{0,1}
RPEt=1−rt+1G
rt+1G=1 Compatiblert+1G=0 Incompatible

Progress, preparation and justified waiting, coordination or recovery can support the task. A task contradiction or unjustified regression or nonprogress does not. The compatible reference reward is 1.

SPE · Execution mismatch

𝒜tAI|H directly specifies acceptable candidate AI actions. φ selects the next-state features used to evaluate execution.

ℰtAI|H={φ(T(st,atH,a)):a∈𝒜tAI|H}
SPEt=𝟙[φ(st+1)∉ℰtAI|H]

For a deterministic transition model, collect the next-state representations produced by all accepted AI actions from the same starting state and human action. SPE is 1 only when the observed representation matches none of them.

Notation and how to read the accepted set +

Task-alignment reward

AI|H means “expected of the AI by the human.” RGAI|H is the task-alignment evaluator, and rt+1G is its binary result. The superscript G identifies goal alignment. This is a normative annotation of task compatibility, separate from the environment’s score and from an estimated human reward expectation.

Accepted next-state representations

a is one candidate AI action. T gives its next state, and φ extracts execution-relevant features. The braces collect these results; the colon means “for each action in 𝒜tAI|H.” ℰtAI|H names the resulting set. 𝟙[·] is 1 if its condition is true, otherwise 0; ∉ means “not in the set.”

For example, if EAST and STAY are both accepted in a particular context, simulate each with the same human action and compare the observed features with either result. No policy probability or probability threshold is required. Different commands can be accepted when they produce the same relevant next state.

Why state features are different from reward +

Reward evaluates task compatibility

A scalar reward answers whether the contribution supports the expected task. Approaching a handoff counter can remain goal-compatible even when the chosen approach unnecessarily blocks the human.

State features retain execution details

φ(st+1) describes what happened: positions, objects, document structure, or other specified features. Keeping only the reward would discard execution differences. RPE and SPE can use overlapping evidence and can both be positive; separate checks do not imply statistical independence.

State and task
The shared draft, instructions and conversation form the state. An AI action is a response, edit or tool call; T updates the shared document. Faithfully summarizing the article satisfies the goal. Asking for genuinely missing source text can be valid preparation.

Execution and labels
A faithful summary in the accepted format: RPE 0 / SPE 0. The same summary in unrequested prose: 0 / 1. Translating instead of summarizing: 1 / 1. φ retains structure, citation preservation and document changes; a validated semantic evaluator or explicit annotation rules replaces exact kitchen-state matching.

Ongoing research explores extending this binary formulation with human models that adapt to dynamically changing preferences during collaboration. Such models could support continuous RPE and SPE values that quantify the degree of mismatch between the AI’s behavior and the human’s evolving expectations about goals and execution.

Task-alignment reward

We compute RGAI|H using rule-based functions applied to the reviewed subtask, game state, and attempted and observed actions. In their defined priority order, these functions check task, item and target consistency; goal-directed progress; and justified preparation, waiting, coordination or recovery. The resulting task-alignment reward is 1 for a compatible contribution and 0 for an incompatible one.

1

Pursues or legitimately supports the expected subtask.

0

Contradicts it or shows unjustified regression or nonprogress.

RPEt=1−rt+1G

Necessary facing, preparation, human-blocked intended progress, legitimate waits and certified yields or detours can remain compatible. Unchanged distance alone does not establish RPE.

Accepted execution

𝒜tAI|H includes alternatives allowed by the target preference and established human passage.

ℰtAI|H={φ(T(st,atH,a)):a∈𝒜tAI|H}
SPEt=𝟙[φ(st+1)∉ℰtAI|H]

φ retains both chefs’ positions, orientations and inventories, world objects and pot states, and remaining orders. Compare these next-state features, rather than command names, animation or game score.

Which task and target apply at each step? +

Follow the reviewed workflow

Use the current reviewed subtask block and physical readiness. Acquiring a plate remains the immediate task until pickup; supplying it starts on the following action. The successful completing interaction belongs to the old block. An erroneous action does not redefine the human’s preferred task.

Separate required and preferred targets

A particular source, handoff counter or pot is a goal constraint when the Hint or reviewed context requires it. Otherwise, targets satisfying the subtask may remain goal-compatible, while the execution preference can still require a preferred target or an accessible human route.

Scope of the formulation and current audit +

Expectations need evidence

A collision alone does not establish a goal error. A wait, farther route or different fixture can be reasonable. The Hint, visible constraints and reviewed preferences establish accepted alternatives. The audit uses saved, current Playback and Subtasks checks for both the evaluated version and its nonPE reference.

Conceptual rule and implementation

The equations summarize the method. The audit adds ordered task checks, reviewed references, readiness, recovery and coordination rules; a reviewed incompatible-input state can establish SPE without an executable recovery probe. Missing evidence remains unresolved. Authored labels and critical/correction markers do not determine step labels.
